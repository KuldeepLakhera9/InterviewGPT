# InterviewGPT — User Personas & Jobs-to-Be-Done (JTBD)

## Overview

To ensure InterviewGPT addresses real candidate frustrations without falling into generic AI traps, we define 4 core personas and map their requirements using the **Jobs-to-Be-Done (JTBD)** framework.

---

## 1. User Personas

### Persona 1: Alex Chen — "The Ambitious New Grad"

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ ALEX CHEN | Age: 22 | Role: CS Graduate Student        │
│ Location: San Jose, CA | Target: L4 Software Engineer   │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

#### Background & Context
- Graduating with a Computer Science B.S. degree.
- Has strong theoretical knowledge (algorithms, data structures) but limited real-world interview practice.
- Gets extremely nervous during live technical rounds and behavioral STAR questions.

#### Core Pain Points
- Freeze-ups during timed technical interviews due to pressure.
- Unsure how to structure answers for behavioral questions like "Tell me about a time you failed."
- Receives standard rejection emails with zero actionable feedback.

#### Desired Outcomes
- Low-stakes, highly realistic environment to practice voice answers.
- Concrete feedback on filler words, pacing, and STAR framework structure.
- Clear step-by-step roadmap to eliminate weak technical areas before upcoming interviews.

### Persona 2: Maya Lin — "The Senior Staff Target"

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ MAYA LIN | Age: 31 | Role: Mid-Level Full Stack Dev    │
│ Location: Austin, TX | Target: Senior / Staff Engineer │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

#### Background & Context
- 6 years of industry software development experience.
- Seeking a step-function promotion to Senior/Staff level at top-tier SaaS companies.
- Needs high-bar System Design and Distributed Systems interview prep.

#### Core Pain Points
- Generic question banks (LeetCode) don't test system architecture tradeoff discussions.
- Hard to find peers with the time or expertise to conduct high-level mock system design interviews.
- Needs realistic pushback from an interviewer when making architectural assumptions (e.g., CAP theorem tradeoffs, database sharding strategies).

#### Desired Outcomes
- Deep, dynamic System Design interviewer that asks probing follow-up questions when trade-offs are omitted.
- Code sandbox testing clean architecture, design patterns, and edge case handling.
- Fast, high-density analytics targeting specific architectural blind spots.

### Persona 3: David Miller — "The Career Switcher"

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ DAVID MILLER | Age: 35 | Role: Former Financial Analyst│
│ Location: Chicago, IL | Target: Frontend / Fullstack   │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

#### Background & Context
- Completed a 6-month intensive coding bootcamp after 10 years in financial analysis.
- Possesses strong domain expertise in fintech/business logic but feels self-conscious about non-traditional background.

#### Core Pain Points
- Struggles to translate financial analysis projects into compelling technical resume bullet points.
- Hard time handling HR screening questions about non-traditional backgrounds.
- Unsure how tech industry interview rubrics differ from finance interviews.

#### Desired Outcomes
- Automated Resume-to-JD matching that identifies missing tech keywords and suggests resume framing.
- HR/Behavioral mock sessions specifically tailored to career switcher narratives.
- Clear guidance on how to highlight transferable project experience.

### Persona 4: Marcus Vance — "University Career Center Director" (B2B Persona)

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ MARCUS VANCE | Age: 48 | Role: Director of Career Dev │
│ Location: Boston, MA | Institution: Tech University    │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

#### Background & Context
- Oversees career outcomes for 3,000+ STEM students annually.
- Limited staff bandwidth to conduct 1-on-1 mock interviews for every graduating student.

#### Core Pain Points
- High student-to-counselor ratio (500:1), making comprehensive mock interview sessions impossible.
- Lack of aggregated metrics on student interview readiness and common skill weaknesses.

#### Desired Outcomes
- Multi-tenant institutional portal where students can perform unassisted AI mock interviews.
- Cohort-level dashboard highlighting aggregate weak spots (e.g., 60% of students struggle with System Design).

## 2. Jobs-to-Be-Done (JTBD) Framework

### JTBD 1: Behavioral & Communication Confidence
> **When I** am preparing for an HR screening or behavioral interview round,  
> **I want to** practice answering questions out loud to a realistic AI interviewer and get immediate feedback on my tone, pacing, and STAR response structure,  
> **So that I can** walk into the real interview feeling confident, articulate, and memorable without stumbling over my words.

### JTBD 2: Technical & Architecture Rigor
> **When I** am targeting a senior technical role with high-bar system design rounds,  
> **I want an** AI interviewer that dynamically challenges my architecture assumptions and asks rigorous follow-up questions based on my specific design choices,  
> **So that I can** sharpen my technical decision-making and handle aggressive follow-ups from Staff engineers.

### JTBD 3: Actionable Weakness Elimination
> **When I** finish a mock interview session,  
> **I want a** precise scorecard detailing exactly which technical concepts or communication habits cost me points, along with an instant practice roadmap,  
> **So that I can** spend my limited study time fixing verified blind spots instead of randomly grinding hundreds of practice problems.

### JTBD 4: Target Resume & JD Alignment
> **When I** apply for a specific role at a company I really care about,  
> **I want to** benchmark my resume against their actual Job Description and run a mock interview customized to that exact company culture and tech stack,  
> **So that I can** maximize my alignment with what their interviewers are explicitly evaluating.
